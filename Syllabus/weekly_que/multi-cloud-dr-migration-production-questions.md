# Multi-Cloud, DR, And Migration Production Question Bank

FAANG-level, Principal/Staff-scenario questions generated from `multi-cloud-dr-migration-week-wise.md`. Pattern-focused: questions compare providers or reason across clouds rather than repeating the single-cloud service catalogs already covered in the AWS, Azure, and GCP question banks. No answers included by design.

## Cross-Cloud Week 1: Cloud Comparison And Provider Selection

1. Your platform team is choosing between GKE Autopilot, EKS with Fargate, and AKS for a new regulated workload — walk through how the identity model differences alone would change your day-2 operational burden.
2. A cost review shows your managed Kubernetes bill is 3x higher on one provider than an equivalent workload on another, despite similar node counts — what would you actually compare before concluding it's a pricing problem versus a configuration problem?
3. A compliance requirement mandates data residency in a region where your primary cloud provider has no availability zone but a secondary provider does — how does this actually change your architecture decision beyond "just use the other provider"?
4. Two teams independently chose different managed database services on two different clouds for similar workloads, and now a merger requires standardizing — what criteria would actually justify keeping both versus consolidating to one?
5. An observability stack comparison shows one provider's native tracing has significantly better service-map fidelity for your microservices topology — is that alone a reason to migrate, or what else would you need to validate first?
6. A vendor's managed database licensing model changes mid-contract in a way that significantly raises costs at your current scale — how do you evaluate whether to renegotiate, migrate, or absorb the cost?
7. Your team's managed Kubernetes upgrade cadence on one provider requires a control-plane maintenance window with brief API unavailability, while another provider claims zero-downtime upgrades — how would you actually validate that claim before trusting it in production?
8. A security posture management tool flags drastically different finding counts for functionally similar infrastructure across two cloud providers — what does this actually tell you, and what doesn't it tell you?
9. A team argues for standardizing all new workloads on a single cloud provider to reduce operational burden, while another argues that provider-native services would be faster to build and cheaper to run — how do you evaluate this tradeoff for a specific workload rather than as a blanket policy?
10. A managed database's backup behavior differs meaningfully between two providers — one supports continuous PITR, the other only daily snapshots at your current tier — how does this change your RPO commitments if you're running the same workload on both?
11. Your team's alerting model on one observability stack is based on log-derived metrics with meaningful ingestion delay, while another provider's native metrics are near-real-time — how would this difference actually show up during an incident?
12. A vendor risk review flags that 80% of your critical infrastructure depends on services only available from a single cloud provider — what would you actually recommend, and what would you explicitly decide not to change?
13. Two managed Kubernetes offerings have different default node operating system patching cadences, and one just shipped an emergency security patch faster than the other — how does this factor into a provider selection decision for a security-sensitive workload?
14. A team's chosen cloud provider has excellent regional availability for compute but limited availability for a specific managed database service your architecture depends on — how do you resolve this constraint?
15. An observability query language difference between two providers means an SRE team's existing incident-response runbooks and saved queries don't transfer directly after a workload migration — how do you weigh this operational cost against the migration's other benefits?
16. A security posture management comparison shows one provider's remediation workflow integrates directly with your ticketing system while another requires a third-party connector that adds latency to fix cycles — how much should this weigh in a provider decision?
17. Your team's skill set is heavily concentrated in one cloud provider, and a new product requirement pushes toward a service only available natively on a competitor — how do you evaluate build-vs-migrate-vs-skill-investment here?
18. A managed database's replication options differ such that one provider supports synchronous multi-zone replication by default while another requires manual configuration for the same guarantee — how does this affect your default RPO assumptions across environments?
19. A merger brings together two teams running equivalent workloads on different cloud providers with different observability retention policies, and a joint postmortem can't reconstruct one team's incident timeline past 14 days — how do you reconcile these standards going forward?
20. A managed Kubernetes provider's node operations model auto-replaces unhealthy nodes without notification, while another requires manual intervention — how does this difference change your on-call runbook design for that platform?
21. A compliance audit requires proof of consistent security control coverage across two cloud providers, but the posture management tools report findings using incompatible severity taxonomies — how do you build a unified compliance narrative from this?
22. A strategic vendor-risk review is being used to justify a multi-cloud strategy, but nobody has quantified the added operational cost of running two provider-specific platforms — how would you build that cost case before committing?

## Cross-Cloud Week 2: Landing Zone And Governance Standardization

1. Your org defines one landing-zone governance model meant to apply identically across AWS, Azure, and GCP, but a policy exception approved for one cloud silently doesn't translate to an equivalent guardrail on another — how do you catch this kind of cross-cloud policy drift?
2. A shared-services layer (logging, identity, network) sits centrally and serves workloads across two cloud providers, and its outage in one region takes down audit evidence collection for both clouds simultaneously — how do you redesign this to reduce that blast radius?
3. An account/subscription/project vending pipeline works reliably for one cloud provider but a manual, ad hoc process still exists for a newer provider your org recently adopted — what governance risk does this create, and how do you close the gap?
4. A tagging/labeling standard defined once at the governance level maps cleanly onto one provider's resource model but only partially onto another's, breaking automated cost reporting for a subset of workloads — how do you handle this structural mismatch?
5. A break-glass access process was designed and tested thoroughly for one cloud provider, but during an actual incident on a different provider, the team discovers no equivalent process was ever built — how do you catch this kind of governance gap before an incident forces it?
6. An audit requires proof that policy inheritance behaves consistently across all three cloud providers' governance hierarchies, but one provider's inheritance model allows a more permissive override at a lower level than the other two — how do you reconcile this in your compliance evidence?
7. A budget guardrail is enforced automatically on two cloud providers but only advisory (non-blocking) on a third due to a platform limitation, and a team on that third provider blows through budget with no automated stop — how do you compensate for this capability gap?
8. Your governance model assumes one consistent environment-isolation pattern (dev/staging/prod) across all providers, but a team on one provider is running dev and staging in the same account/subscription due to historical setup — how do you bring this back into alignment without a disruptive migration?
9. A shared logging baseline is meant to centralize audit evidence from all three clouds, but retention policy was only explicitly configured for the two clouds your org adopted first, leaving the third cloud's logs on default retention — how do you catch this kind of silent gap?
10. An exception to a governance guardrail was granted on one cloud provider "temporarily" during a migration eighteen months ago and was never revisited — how do you build an exception-lifecycle process that would have caught this?
11. A newly onboarded team requests the standard governance baseline, but their workload's cloud provider doesn't support one of the guardrails your governance model assumes is universal — how do you handle this without weakening the model for everyone else?
12. An incident response team needs operational access across two cloud providers during a cross-cloud outage, but each provider's break-glass process requires a different approval chain, costing critical minutes — how do you design for this?
13. A drift-detection process catches configuration drift reliably on your primary cloud provider but has much weaker coverage on a secondary provider your org uses for a smaller subset of workloads — how do you prioritize closing this gap?
14. Two teams on two different cloud providers both claim to follow the same environment-isolation governance model, but a security review finds meaningfully different actual isolation guarantees between them — what would you investigate to find where the model's provider-specific implementation diverged from intent?
15. A cost baseline meant to be provider-neutral doesn't account for one provider's markedly different data-egress pricing model, and a workload migrated for "cost savings" ends up more expensive after including egress — how do you build cost baselines that would catch this before migration?
16. An IAM boundary defined once at the governance level ("no standing production access without justification") is enforced through fundamentally different mechanisms on each provider, and an audit can't easily prove equivalent enforcement across all three — how do you build evidence that satisfies this?
17. A shared-services subscription/account/project hosting cross-cloud connectivity infrastructure becomes a single point of governance failure when its own audit logging silently stops, and nobody notices for weeks — how do you monitor the monitors here?
18. Your governance model was designed assuming three clouds, but a fourth provider was quietly adopted by one team for a specific managed service unavailable elsewhere — how do you bring this back under the standard governance umbrella?
19. A compliance mapping exercise finds that one cloud provider's native policy engine can enforce a control automatically while the equivalent control on another provider only has manual, auditable evidence — how does this affect your risk posture, and how do you communicate it?
20. A platform blast-radius review finds that a single shared network baseline component, if misconfigured, could simultaneously affect workloads across all three cloud providers — how do you redesign for isolation without losing the benefit of centralized governance?

## Cross-Cloud Week 3: Network, Identity, And Private Connectivity

1. Your org runs a hub-spoke network topology on one cloud provider and a flatter shared-VPC-style model on another, and a security incident needs consistent east-west traffic inspection across both — how do you reconcile these fundamentally different topologies for incident response?
2. A private connectivity link between your on-prem data center and two different cloud providers shows very different latency characteristics for functionally similar workloads — what would you actually investigate before assuming one provider's network path is just slower?
3. A DNS split-brain scenario emerges where an internal service name resolves to different, both-technically-valid endpoints depending on which cloud provider's network the client is on — how do you diagnose and design around this?
4. Two cloud providers' native firewall/security-group models have different default-deny behaviors, and a firewall-rule "translation" during a migration silently left one path more permissive than intended — how do you catch this class of translation error?
5. A service running in Cloud A needs to authenticate securely to a service running in Cloud B without relying on static credentials — how do you design cross-cloud service-to-service identity so this doesn't come down to a shared secret sitting in a config file?
6. An asymmetric routing issue between two cloud providers' interconnected networks causes intermittent connection resets that only show up under specific traffic patterns — how do you isolate this without full packet capture on both sides?
7. A NAT exhaustion issue on one cloud provider's network only appears during a specific batch job's peak concurrency, and the equivalent workload on another provider has never hit this limit — what would you compare between the two NAT implementations to explain the difference?
8. A staged DNS cutover during a cross-cloud migration leaves a subset of traffic resolving to the old environment for several days due to a client-side caching layer nobody accounted for — how do you build a cutover plan that catches this before it becomes a customer-facing gap?
9. Your org's private connectivity between two cloud providers depends on a third-party network provider whose own maintenance window isn't visible in either cloud's status dashboards — how do you build failure detection for a dependency you don't directly control?
10. An overlapping CIDR range between an on-prem network and a newly connected second cloud provider's VPC/VNet is discovered only after initial connectivity was established — what are your realistic remediation options without a full re-IP?
11. A certificate issued by one cloud provider's managed certificate service isn't trusted by a service running on another provider without additional configuration, and this wasn't caught until a cross-cloud integration went live — how do you design certificate trust for cross-cloud service communication going forward?
12. A WAF rule set tuned carefully for traffic patterns on one cloud provider's edge service is copied to another provider's equivalent WAF during a migration, and it starts blocking legitimate traffic almost immediately — what would explain this, and how do you validate WAF rules during cross-cloud migration?
13. An egress control policy enforced consistently on one cloud provider has a documented but easy-to-miss exception path on another provider, and a workload is unintentionally exfiltrating more data than intended through that path — how do you audit for this kind of policy mismatch?
14. A route-propagation failure between an on-prem network and a cloud provider's private connectivity service causes a specific subnet to silently lose reachability for six hours before anyone notices — how do you build detection for this kind of silent route drift?
15. Two clouds' identity federation setups for service-to-service authentication use different token lifetimes and refresh behaviors, and a long-running cross-cloud batch job starts failing mid-run after several hours — how do you diagnose this class of failure?
16. A staged traffic-cutover plan for a network migration assumes symmetric bandwidth between old and new paths, but the new cross-cloud path has materially lower throughput under real load — how do you validate this assumption before committing to the cutover?
17. An ingress pattern that terminates TLS at one cloud provider's load balancer and re-encrypts to the backend works differently from another provider's equivalent service, and a security review flags unencrypted traffic on an internal hop during a migration — how do you catch this kind of gap systematically?
18. A hub-spoke network's centralized inspection point becomes a shared point of failure when a security appliance in the hub goes down, taking down connectivity for every spoke across multiple cloud accounts — how do you design for this without losing centralized inspection?
19. A migration introduces identity federation between an on-prem IdP and two different cloud providers simultaneously, and a permission-mapping error grants broader access on one provider than intended — how do you validate federation mappings before they reach production?
20. A firewall rule translation tool used during a migration maps rules from one provider's model to another's but silently drops a rule type that has no direct equivalent — how do you build validation that catches these silent gaps rather than discovering them from an incident?

## Cross-Cloud Week 4: Migration Strategy And Execution

1. A migration assessment's application inventory misses an undocumented dependency between two services, and this isn't discovered until a wave-3 cutover breaks a service that should have been in wave 1 — how do you build dependency mapping that would have caught this?
2. A team chooses "rehost" (lift-and-shift) for a workload to hit a deadline, planning to "replatform later," but eighteen months later that replatforming has never happened and the workload now carries the operational cost of both approaches — how do you prevent migration-pattern decisions from silently becoming permanent?
3. A low-risk pilot wave completes successfully, but the pilot workload turns out to be architecturally unrepresentative of the business-critical services planned for later waves, and the team discovers integration issues only in wave 4 — how do you choose pilot workloads that actually validate the riskiest assumptions?
4. A secrets migration during a platform cutover leaves a subset of secrets duplicated in both the old and new secret stores with no clear source of truth, and a rotation event updates one but not the other — how do you design secrets migration to avoid this split-brain state?
5. An IaC state migration between platforms loses track of a handful of resources that were created manually outside the original IaC process, and those resources are accidentally destroyed during cutover — how do you validate IaC state completeness before a migration cutover?
6. A "retire" decision was made for a legacy service during migration assessment, but a downstream team was quietly still depending on one of its endpoints, and this isn't discovered until after decommissioning — how do you build a dependency-verification process that would catch this before retirement?
7. A GitOps cutover to a new platform goes live, but the old platform's GitOps controller wasn't fully disabled, and both systems briefly reconcile conflicting state to the same production environment — how do you sequence a GitOps cutover to prevent this?
8. A data-reconciliation check after a stateful migration wave only validates row counts, not actual data correctness, and a subtle data-type conversion bug isn't caught until a downstream financial report shows discrepancies weeks later — how do you design reconciliation that would catch this?
9. A wave-planning exercise groups services by team ownership rather than technical dependency, and a rollback of one wave turns out to be impossible without also rolling back a dependent service in a different wave — how do you structure rollback groups to avoid this?
10. A migration's synthetic tests validate that a service responds correctly post-cutover, but don't validate response times under realistic concurrent load, and a latency regression only appears during the first real traffic spike — how do you design synthetic validation that would catch this?
11. An artifact registry migration changes image digests even for functionally identical images, breaking a deployment pipeline's pinned-digest references across dozens of services simultaneously — how do you plan an artifact registry migration to avoid this class of breakage?
12. A business-critical path was migrated last, per standard wave-planning wisdom, but this means the team has the least cutover experience and the least time pressure discipline exactly when the stakes are highest — how do you address this tension in wave sequencing?
13. A CI/CD platform migration changes how secrets are injected into build pipelines, and a build that previously worked starts silently using a stale cached secret instead of failing loudly — how do you validate CI/CD migrations catch this kind of silent failure mode?
14. A migration's compliance-constraint assessment correctly identifies a data-residency requirement, but the wave plan doesn't account for the fact that a shared logging pipeline would route that region's logs through a non-compliant region during cutover — how do you catch this kind of transitive compliance gap?
15. An observability migration moves dashboards and alerts to a new platform, but historical baseline data doesn't migrate with it, so post-cutover anomaly detection has no historical context and either stays silent or fires constantly — how do you handle this transition period?
16. A rollback criterion was defined as "error rate below X%" for a migration wave, but the actual production failure mode after cutover was a slow memory leak that wouldn't breach that threshold for hours — how do you define rollback criteria that would catch this kind of slow-building failure?
17. A "repurchase" decision (replacing a self-managed system with a SaaS equivalent) during migration assessment didn't account for a compliance requirement the SaaS vendor can't meet, and this is discovered only after the migration wave is already underway — how do you validate vendor compliance fit earlier in the assessment?
18. A performance baseline captured before migration was taken during a low-traffic period, and post-cutover performance looks like a regression when it's actually just normal peak-traffic behavior nobody baselined — how do you build baselines that avoid this false positive?
19. Business signoff for a migration wave was obtained based on a demo environment that didn't reflect production data volume, and the actual cutover reveals scaling issues the demo never surfaced — how do you structure signoff criteria to avoid this gap?
20. A "relocate" migration pattern (moving a workload without changing its architecture, e.g., between regions of the same provider) is assumed to be low-risk, but a team discovers a hardcoded regional dependency only during the cutover itself — how do you build assessment steps that would have caught this before calling it low-risk?

## Cross-Cloud Week 5: DR, HA, And Active-Active Decision Making

1. A business unit insists on active-active across two regions for cost-justification reasons, but nobody has actually calculated whether the added complexity and conflict-handling cost exceeds the RTO improvement over a well-tested warm-standby pattern — how do you build the case to challenge this decision?
2. A pilot-light DR pattern's documented RTO assumes standby infrastructure can be scaled up in minutes, but a game day reveals the actual scale-up time is hours due to a dependency nobody accounted for — how do you validate RTO assumptions before trusting them?
3. A global load balancer's regional health checks report a region as healthy throughout an incident that real users clearly experienced as down — what gap in health-check design would explain this, regardless of which cloud provider is involved?
4. An active-passive failover completes successfully at the infrastructure layer, but client-side retry behavior means a meaningful fraction of in-flight requests are lost rather than retried against the new active region — how do you design for this at the client layer?
5. A conflict-handling strategy for an active-active data layer wasn't actually tested under a real network partition, and the first real partition event produces silently divergent data that isn't caught for days — how do you validate conflict handling before trusting an active-active claim?
6. A DR runbook's manual override procedure for forcing a failover exists on paper, but during an actual regional incident, the person who knows how to execute it is unreachable — how do you design for this single point of human failure?
7. A backup-restore DR pattern's documented RPO assumes backups complete on schedule, but a game day reveals the last successful backup was actually 6 hours stale due to a silent failure nobody was alerted to — how do you build monitoring that would have caught this before it mattered?
8. An immutable storage configuration for backups was assumed to protect against a ransomware scenario, but the immutability policy itself was applied at a scope that an attacker with sufficient access could still modify — how do you validate that immutability actually holds under a realistic threat model?
9. A data-residency requirement conflicts with a DR strategy that would normally replicate data to the nearest available region, and the team only discovers this conflict during a DR test, not during design — how do you catch this kind of conflict earlier?
10. A warm-standby architecture's failover time was measured during a controlled game day with full staffing and advance notice, and the actual failover during a real 3am incident takes significantly longer — how do you build DR testing that's representative of real conditions?
11. A KMS/key-recovery process for DR assumes keys are accessible in the DR region, but during an actual regional outage, the key management service itself becomes part of the affected blast radius — how do you redesign key architecture to avoid this circular dependency?
12. A customer-impact validation process for a DR failover only checks that services respond, not that they respond correctly with consistent data, and a failover is declared successful while actually serving stale or incorrect data to some users — how do you build validation that would catch this?
13. An emergency access procedure for DR events grants broad temporary access, but audit trails for that access are incomplete because actions were taken directly against infrastructure rather than through the normal change process — how do you preserve audit quality without slowing down emergency response?
14. A DNS-failover-based traffic pattern is undermined by client-side DNS caching with a longer TTL than the failover plan assumed, and a meaningful fraction of traffic doesn't fail over within the target RTO — how do you address this without controlling every client's caching behavior?
15. A rollback-routing plan for a failed failover assumes the original primary region is still fully intact and ready to receive traffic back, but configuration drift accumulated during the incident window means it isn't — how do you design for safe rollback under this uncertainty?
16. A PITR-based recovery is tested and declared successful because the restore operation completed, but nobody validated that the recovered data actually matches the intended recovery point — how do you build PITR validation that goes beyond "the restore didn't error"?
17. A business-tiering exercise classifies a service as lower-priority for DR investment, but a later incident reveals that service is a silent dependency for several higher-tier services — how do you validate business-tiering assumptions against actual technical dependencies?
18. A dependency-readiness check before a DR failover test confirms the primary services are ready, but doesn't check that supporting services (DNS, certificate management, secrets) are equally ready in the DR environment — how do you build a dependency-readiness check that's actually comprehensive?
19. An active-active architecture's cost was justified partly by eliminating failover time entirely, but nobody accounted for the ongoing operational cost of maintaining conflict-resolution logic and testing it continuously — how do you build a realistic total-cost comparison against alternatives?
20. A ransomware-recovery plan assumes backup access credentials are separate from production credentials, but an audit finds they actually share a common privileged identity that was compromised in a simulated attack — how do you validate this separation actually holds rather than just being documented?

## Cross-Cloud Week 6: Cloud Security, Compliance, And Incident Response

1. An incident spans workloads on two cloud providers simultaneously, and each provider's audit log has a different timestamp precision and time zone default, making it genuinely difficult to establish a single authoritative timeline — how do you build an incident response process that handles this?
2. A workload identity federation trust relationship between an on-prem system and two cloud providers was set up correctly at first, but a routine credential rotation on the on-prem side silently broke federation to only one of the two clouds — how do you catch this kind of partial breakage?
3. A posture management tool suppresses a finding as a known exception, but the underlying exception was only ever meant to be temporary and its expiry was never enforced — how do you build an exception-governance process that prevents this from becoming permanent by default?
4. During a live incident, containment requires revoking credentials across two cloud providers simultaneously, but the team only has a well-tested runbook for one provider, and manual, error-prone steps are used for the other — how do you close this kind of runbook gap before the next incident?
5. A compliance audit requires proof that least-privilege access reviews happen consistently across three cloud providers, but each provider's access-review tooling produces evidence in a different format with different levels of granularity — how do you build a unified compliance narrative from this?
6. A migration's temporary elevated access, granted to speed up a cutover, is never explicitly revoked afterward because no formal process tracked its expiry — how do you design temporary access so it can't silently become permanent?
7. A break-glass credential used during a cross-cloud incident on one provider turns out to have much broader effective permissions than intended, due to a privilege-escalation path the team wasn't aware of — how do you validate break-glass scope before you need it in an emergency?
8. A regulatory notification deadline requires customer communication within a specific window after a data-impacting incident, but the incident actually started on one cloud provider and only became visible when it propagated to a second provider days later — how does this affect your interpretation of "when the incident started"?
9. A vulnerability remediation SLA is tracked consistently on one cloud provider's posture management tool but a newer, less mature tool on a second provider has significant reporting gaps, understating actual remediation age — how do you validate remediation-age data before trusting it for compliance reporting?
10. During a migration's dual-run period, both the old and new environments have live production access simultaneously, and a security review finds this doubled the attack surface for several weeks with nobody explicitly accepting that risk — how do you make dual-run exposure an explicit, time-boxed decision rather than an accidental one?
11. An evidence-preservation step during incident response captures logs and configuration state from one cloud provider promptly, but by the time the team gets to a second provider involved in the same incident, some ephemeral evidence has already expired due to shorter default retention — how do you design evidence preservation for a genuinely cross-cloud incident?
12. A source-environment hardening step was skipped during a migration because the source system was "being decommissioned soon anyway," but the decommissioning slipped by eight months, leaving a known-vulnerable system in production far longer than planned — how do you prevent hardening shortcuts from becoming long-term risk?
13. A post-cutover cleanup checklist for a migration doesn't include revoking cross-cloud federation trust that was only needed during the migration window, and this trust relationship is discovered still active a year later during a security review — how do you build cleanup processes that reliably catch this?
14. An access review process across three cloud providers relies on each provider's native reporting, but one provider's report doesn't distinguish between human and service-account access in a way that lets you actually validate least privilege — how do you work around this reporting gap?
15. A credential-rotation process during incident containment successfully rotates a compromised credential on one cloud provider, but a cached copy of the old credential in a CI/CD system on a different provider continues to work for several more hours — how do you build containment that accounts for credential propagation you don't directly control?
16. A policy-as-code framework enforces a control consistently on two cloud providers but has no equivalent enforcement mechanism on a third, where the same control exists only as a manually-audited checklist item — how does this affect your actual confidence in that control, versus your documented confidence?
17. Network isolation during incident containment is straightforward on one cloud provider's network model but much harder to achieve cleanly on another provider's flatter network topology without also isolating unrelated workloads — how do you plan containment strategies that account for this asymmetry in advance?
18. A compliance mapping exercise assumes encryption-at-rest is enabled by default across all cloud resources, but an audit finds a specific resource type on one provider that requires explicit opt-in and was missed — how do you build verification that catches gaps like this systematically rather than by assumption?
19. A data-transfer protection control encrypts data in transit between two cloud providers during a migration, but a review finds the encryption terminates at a network appliance that logs plaintext payloads for debugging purposes — how do you audit for this kind of protection gap that looks compliant on paper?
20. An ownership-proof requirement for compliance asks who is accountable for a specific control across all three cloud providers, but the actual answer differs by provider due to historical team structure, and no single document reflects the current reality — how do you build ownership evidence that stays accurate as teams change?

## Cross-Cloud Week 7: FinOps, Reliability, And Executive Reporting

1. A cost-anomaly detection system flags a spend spike three weeks after it started because the anomaly threshold was tuned to avoid false positives during a known seasonal traffic pattern — how do you redesign anomaly detection to catch genuine anomalies faster without reintroducing the false-positive problem?
2. A committed-use/reserved-capacity purchase was sized based on last year's growth projections, and actual usage came in significantly under that commitment, locking in wasted spend for the remainder of the term — how do you build a committed-use planning process that's more resilient to forecast error?
3. A spot/preemptible instance strategy saves significant cost on paper, but a reliability report six months later shows this strategy is quietly responsible for a disproportionate share of production incidents — how do you build cost and reliability reporting that would surface this tradeoff earlier?
4. A storage-lifecycle policy moves data to a cheaper tier to reduce cost, and this shows up as a clean win in the FinOps report, but a separate reliability report doesn't capture that a critical batch job's runtime tripled as a result — how do you connect cost and reliability reporting so tradeoffs like this are visible together?
5. Data-transfer costs between two environments grow steadily over several quarters without anyone noticing until a cost review flags it, because no single dashboard tracked this cost category as its own line item — how do you build cost observability that would have caught this trend earlier?
6. An MTTR metric looks like it's improving quarter over quarter, but the underlying incident severity mix has quietly shifted toward less severe incidents, making the metric look better without operational practice actually improving — how do you build reliability reporting that would catch this kind of metric drift?
7. A change-failure-rate metric is reported favorably because it only counts failures caught in production, not failures caught and rolled back during a canary phase — how do you decide what should actually count toward this metric to make it meaningful?
8. An executive investment ask for additional DR spend cites "improved RTO" as the benefit, but nobody has quantified what a missed RTO actually costs the business in the current state — how do you build the business case with measurable terms rather than technical terms alone?
9. A migration's risk register flagged a specific cutover as high-risk months in advance, but the risk was never escalated to an executive decision-maker until the week of cutover, leaving no real time to change course — how do you design risk reporting that surfaces material risks early enough to matter?
10. A capacity-headroom report shows healthy aggregate headroom across a fleet, but a specific critical service is quietly running much closer to its limit than the aggregate suggests — how do you build capacity reporting that wouldn't mask this kind of concentrated risk?
11. A cost-variance report for a migration shows the project came in on budget overall, but this masks that one specific workload came in significantly over budget while others came in under, information that would have changed a go/no-go decision if seen earlier — how do you build cost-variance reporting granular enough to catch this?
12. An observability tooling cost has grown to a meaningful fraction of total infrastructure spend, and a review finds a large share of that cost is logs nobody has queried in over a year — how do you build an ongoing process to right-size observability spend without losing genuinely needed data?
13. A DR-readiness report marks a service as "ready" based on the existence of a documented runbook, without any recent validation that the runbook actually works — how do you redesign DR-readiness reporting to reflect tested readiness rather than documented readiness?
14. An executive risk-acceptance decision was made based on a dependency-risk report that significantly understated how many downstream services actually depend on a specific piece of aging infrastructure — how do you build dependency-risk reporting that's accurate enough to support this kind of decision?
15. A decision record for choosing a specific migration pattern for a major workload was never actually written down, and eighteen months later nobody can reconstruct why that decision was made when the assumptions behind it have since changed — how do you build a decision-record practice that would prevent this?
16. A wave-status report for an ongoing migration shows waves as "complete" based on deployment, but a later audit finds several waves never actually completed their planned post-cutover validation steps — how do you redesign wave-status reporting to distinguish "deployed" from "actually validated"?
17. An SLO-based reliability report shows a service meeting its target, but the SLO itself was defined years ago for a much smaller scale and no longer reflects what actually matters to current customers — how do you build a process for keeping SLO definitions current as a business evolves?
18. A budget guardrail alert fires only after a team has already exceeded their monthly budget, at a point too late to meaningfully change behavior for that cycle — how do you redesign budget reporting to be actionable earlier in the cycle?
19. An executive report on migration defects counts only defects found in production, not defects caught during validation, making a genuinely risky migration wave look cleaner than it was — how do you decide what belongs in defect reporting to give an accurate risk picture?
20. A rollback-readiness claim for a migration wave was based on the rollback procedure existing, not on it ever having been tested, and when a rollback is actually needed, the procedure fails in a way that extends the outage — how do you build rollback-readiness validation into your standard process?
